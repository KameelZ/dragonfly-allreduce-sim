Idea #2 (hierarchical schedule, test_8) already closes the gap to parity by cutting cross-leaf message volume. Idea #5 (multipath, test_11) spreads whatever volume remains across spines instead of funneling it through one. This tests whether combining both -- less traffic, spread across more paths -- is enough to push DF+ past parity into an outright win, which neither change alone was expected to achieve on its own (idea #5 alone cannot remove test_6's fixed extra-hop latency cost; idea #2 alone still funnels its reduced traffic through a single spine without this fix).

Optimization idea #2: g-PAARD with steps 3/4's flat O(hosts^2) intra-group any-to-all replaced by a 2-level hierarchy -- combine within each leaf/router first (cheap, same cost in DF and DF+), then exchange among one representative per leaf only (the only part that crosses DF+'s expensive leaf-spine hop, now O(leaves^2) instead of O(hosts^2)), then broadcast back. test_7's spine-oversubscription triage showed hardware alone can't fix the gap (no ECMP in this project's routing); this targets the same contention by cutting message volume instead.
